# Rubber Trees

## Renewable Industrial Compliance

After learning that steel does not grow on trees, GenTech's Arboricultural Materials Division redirected its remaining
budget toward something that does. **Rubber Trees** provide logs, planks, saplings, and valuable Tree Sap while requiring
only soil, time, and a continuing commitment to responsible deforestation.

Rubber Trees may be found growing naturally throughout many plains, forests, swamps, meadows, and windswept regions. Their
saplings can be replanted and grown like ordinary trees, allowing every harvested woodland to become a future harvested
woodland.

## Approved Sap Acquisition Procedure

Upright Rubber Logs gradually accumulate sap. When a log is ready for collection, a visible resin patch appears on one of
its sides. GenTech recommends the following certified harvesting process:

1. Locate a mature Rubber Log displaying a resin patch.
2. Cut the log using a **Saw**.
3. Submit a positive attitude while awaiting results.
4. Recover 1–2 Tree Sap if the approved 50% collection chance is successful.
5. Replant the recovered sapling so the Forestry Division may continue describing the process as renewable.

When the Saw breaks a log, it automatically fells connected logs above it. Crouch while cutting if you intend to remove
only one log and would prefer the rest of the tree to remain employed. Sawing logs and planks may also produce Sawdust.

## Wood Products

Rubber Logs and Rubber Wood can be stripped with an axe or Saw for approved decorative applications. One Rubber Log may
also be crafted into four Rubber Planks, suitable for construction projects that require wood with a more industrial name.

> **GENTECH MATERIAL SAFETY NOTICE:** Despite several promising interpretations of the word “rubber,” Rubber Logs, Rubber
> Wood, and Rubber Planks are flammable. Keep them away from Heating Elements, open flames, and interns attempting to
> validate either of the previous warnings.

GenTech thanks you for participating in the cycle of planting, waiting, harvesting, and taking credit for nature's work.
