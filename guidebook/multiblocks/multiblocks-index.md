# Multiblocks

## Industrial Ambition, Now Several Blocks Wide

Single-block machines are compact, practical, and tragically limited by their refusal to occupy an entire department.
GenTech **multiblocks** combine controllers, casings, specialized components, and carefully assigned empty space into
industrial structures capable of delivering larger results and substantially larger construction errors.

Every multiblock must match its approved structure before the controller will operate. If a machine refuses to form,
inspect its orientation, required components, internal spaces, and hatches before submitting a complaint to the laws of
geometry.

## Shared Walls

In response to increasing material costs and decreasing available floor space, GenTech authorizes compatible multiblocks
to share ordinary casing blocks across one or more walls. This practice allows the same casing to appear on multiple
construction reports and is therefore considered highly efficient.

Controller blocks cannot be shared. Each controller defines and manages its own multiblock, maintains its own resources,
and remains unwilling to recognize a joint leadership structure.

Hatches may be physically placed in a shared wall, but each hatch belongs to exactly one controller. If the hatch already
has an owner, that ownership is preserved. If an unbound hatch could belong to multiple multiblocks, one controller is
selected as its owner. Every other multiblock attempting to use that hatch becomes invalid and cannot process recipes or
open its interface.

To keep every adjacent multiblock valid, place each machine's hatches on casing positions that are **not shared** with
another multiblock. Walls may participate in cooperative ownership. Access points may not.

> **GENTECH PROPERTY NOTICE:** Sharing a wall reduces construction materials. Sharing a hatch reduces functioning factories.
